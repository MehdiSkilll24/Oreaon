Viktor Tsoi was a Soviet singer-songwriter and actor who co-founded Kino, one of the most influential bands in Russian music history. Born in 1962 to a Russian mother and Korean father in Leningrad, he started writing songs as a teenager and became a prominent figure in the popular band Kino. Tsoi contributed ten albums throughout his career and starred in the 1988 Kazakh new wave art film "The Needle". In 1990, after a high-profile concert at Moscow's Luzhniki Stadium, he moved to Latvia with bandmate Yuri Kasparyan for their next album. Tsoi died in a traffic collision two months later at the age of 28.
